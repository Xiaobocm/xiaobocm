Learning Three Languages While Learning Everything Else

English is the tool I need. Japanese and Korean are the doors I chose.

I am a Grade 9 student in Ningbo. Most of my day is already spoken for: school, homework, the small number of hours left over. Learning languages is not a separate life. It has to fit inside this one.

English as infrastructure

Most of the technical world I want to enter speaks English. Documentation, papers, issue threads, conference talks, the strange shorthand of open source. If I want to understand how a model works, I need more than translated summaries. I need the original room.

So English is not a hobby for me. It is infrastructure. I practice it by reading what I actually want to read: release notes, blog posts, code comments, and the occasional argument about whether a benchmark means anything.

Japanese and Korean as play

Japanese and Korean are different. I am learning them because I like them. That freedom matters. There is no exam, no deadline, no one checking whether I am “good enough.” I can follow a song, recognize a phrase, laugh at a subtitle that lands differently in the original.

Play is underrated in learning. It keeps the door open when progress is slow.

The LLM shortcut trap

I use language models to explain grammar and generate examples. I try not to use them to skip the struggle. If I ask a model to translate every sentence, I am practicing the model’s Chinese-to-English, not my own.

The useful version is a loop: try, get corrected, try again. The model is a patient tutor, not a substitute for attention.

Small loops

My practice is not impressive. Fifteen minutes of input. A few sentences out loud. One new word used in a real sentence. A short recording of my voice, played back with the volume turned up and the embarrassment turned down.

The goal is not to finish. It is to stay in contact.

Why it matters

Language is a second interface to ideas. It changes what you can notice, who you can learn from, and how many versions of a thought you can hold at once. For someone interested in AI, that is not a soft skill. It is a research skill.

Fluency is a direction, not a finish line. I am still walking.
