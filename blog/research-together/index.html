What Co-Founding SinoFlare Taught Me About Research

SinoFlare started as a small collective of people who wanted to do research together. I co-founded it with peers, and it has become one of the most useful parts of my education.

Not because we have all the answers. Because we have a room — sometimes a chat, sometimes a call — where a half-formed idea can be taken seriously without being treated as finished.

Research is a social activity

We talk about research as if it happens inside one person’s head. Sometimes it does. But most of the work is external: explaining a problem, noticing a gap in someone else’s explanation, borrowing a method from a different field, being told that your beautiful assumption is wrong.

A good collaborator does not just agree with you. They make your idea more precise.

Half-formed ideas need a room

The most valuable thing about SinoFlare is the permission to be early. You can bring a note that is 40% formed and say, “I think there is something here.” The group’s job is not to clap. It is to ask what “here” means.

That is harder than it sounds. School rewards finished answers. Research rewards good questions, and good questions usually arrive before the vocabulary to ask them properly.

The discipline of explaining

Writing is how I find out what I actually think. A paragraph that felt clear in my head often falls apart on the page. That is useful. It shows me which part was a feeling and which part was an argument.

SinoFlare gives me an audience for that process: small enough to be honest, serious enough to care.

Momentum over intensity

We are students. There are weeks when school takes everything. The collective works because we do not require constant intensity. We require momentum: a note, a link, a question, a small experiment. Something that keeps the thread alive.

That is also a lesson about research in general. Consistency beats bursts. A hundred small observations can become a paper. Ten brilliant weekends usually become ten abandoned folders.

What I want it to become

I want SinoFlare to be a place where the work is legible and the people are generous. Not a hype machine. Not a resume decoration. A small group that gets better at asking, building, and checking.

If that sounds like your kind of thing, the door is open. Bring a question.
